Chinese business gift etiquette

By the 585555 Editorial Team · Updated {{ site.time | date: "%B %Y" }} · 7 min read

A thoughtful gift builds 关系 (guānxi, relationships). A careless one can end a deal before it starts.

Safe winners

  • Premium tea (pu'er, oolong, longjing) in a presentation box.
  • Quality wine or spirits for banquet-centric relationships.
  • Mooncakes at Mid-Autumn; mandarin oranges at New Year (in pairs).
  • Well-made items from your home region — they carry a story.

Never give

  • Clocks (送钟 = attending a death), umbrellas (separation), pears (parting), knives/scissors (cutting ties).
  • Green hats, white flowers, anything in fours, cash in white envelopes.

The ritual

  1. Present with both hands, slightly bowing your head.
  2. Expect the gift to be declined once or twice — insist politely.
  3. Don't expect it to be opened in front of you.
  4. Give the most senior person the best gift; equalise for the rest of the team.
  5. Reciprocate — a gift received creates an expectation.

Compliance

Mainland Chinese officials and state-owned enterprise staff face strict anti-graft rules. Many multinationals set gift limits under their own anti-bribery policies (e.g. under the US FCPA or UK Bribery Act). Keep gifts modest, branded and recorded. This is general information, not legal advice.
