Why Chinese buyers value numeric domains

By the 585555 Editorial Team · Updated {{UPDATED}} · 7 min read

For hundreds of millions of Chinese internet users, numbers are easier to remember and type than English words — and they can carry meaning of their own. That makes short numeric .com names a real market.

Why numbers work online in China

  • Language-neutral: digits are universal; English words and even pinyin aren't.
  • Meaningful: 58 = "I prosper", 520 = "I love you", 51 = "I want".
  • Memorable: famous examples include 163.com, 360.cn and 58.com (each a trademark of its owner).

How the market classifies them

What moves value

  1. No 4s. Among Chinese buyers, a single 4 can sharply reduce demand.
  2. 8s and 6s add premium, especially as endings.
  3. Repetition (AAAA, AABB) improves memorability.
  4. Meaning — a readable phrase like 518 or 168 beats random digits.
  5. Length — shorter is scarcer.

Buying or selling safely

  • Use a reputable escrow service; never transfer before funds clear.
  • Check the name's history (archives, blacklists) and trademark conflicts before purchase.
  • Agree on registrar push vs. auth-code transfer in writing.

This is general information, not investment advice. Domain prices are volatile.
